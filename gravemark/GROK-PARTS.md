# Gravemark — parts brief

_Generated by `tools/grok-parts.js` from the rig tables and a live scan of `art/`. Regenerate after every delivery._

**271 parts across 32 characters.** Tiered — Tier 1 alone puts a fully animated, gear-visible hero in the game.

## What this is, and why it is the ask that finally works

Path of Idle's characters are painted **once, as separate body parts**, and a bone rig moves the parts. The art never changes; the skeleton does. That rig is now built into Gravemark, with its animations — idle, five weapon swings, hit, death, walk, plus monster attacks — authored in code. What it is missing is the parts to wear.

An image generator cannot animate. But it can absolutely **paint one piece of a figure from a reference painting**, and that is all this brief asks for. Every part is a single still image. No sheets, no frames, no motion — the motion is already done.

Once the hero's parts exist, **equipped gear becomes visible on the animated character**: the hero is painted at three gear bands (low / mid / high) and the game swaps the set to match what the squad is wearing.

## The convention — every part follows this exactly

1. **Attach the character's reference painting** to every prompt. The part must match it in style, lighting, colour and scale — it is a *crop* of that figure, not a new drawing.
2. **Paint the part UPRIGHT, exactly as it sits on the reference in its resting pose.** Do not rotate it, straighten it, or pose it. The rig rotates parts itself; at rest it applies no rotation at all, which is why "as it appears on the reference" is the whole instruction.
3. **Only that part.** A forearm is elbow-to-fingertips and nothing else. No shoulder, no shadow of the torso, no ground.
4. **Fill the canvas** given for the part, with the named **joint at the given position**. The joint is where the bone pivots — the shoulder for an upper arm, the hips for the torso — and the rig hangs the part from that exact point. Get this wrong and the arm detaches at the shoulder when it swings.
5. **Flat magenta `#FF00FF` background**, edge to edge. Never ask for transparency; it comes back as a white card.
6. **No text, no watermark, no border.**

Near-side parts (`_f`) are the ones closer to the viewer. Far-side parts (`_b`) are the same limb on the other side of the body — paint them a touch darker, they are further from the light.

### The prompt template

```
[attach the reference painting]

From the attached reference painting of <character>, paint ONLY <part description>,
exactly as it appears on the reference: same style, same lighting, same colours, same
scale, upright, not re-posed. Nothing else of the body in the image.

Crop it to fill a <W>x<H> pixel canvas, with <joint> positioned at <X%> across and
<Y%> down the canvas.

Flat solid magenta #FF00FF background edge to edge. No text, no watermark, no border.
```

## Style block — paste above every prompt

```
Hand-painted 2D game art, painterly with visible brush texture. Cold, damp, cut stone.
NOT vector, NOT pixel art, NOT 3D render, NOT cartoon.
One low warm key light against cold blue-grey ambient, rim light on the edges.
Dark overall; bone #e6e0cf is the brightest neutral; ONE saturated accent per character.
This image is a CROP OF THE ATTACHED REFERENCE and must match it exactly.
```

## Delivery

- **271 images** across five tiers. Partial deliveries land the same day: a finished Tier 1 is an animated hero.
- One PNG per part, named exactly as the key: `parts/hero-mid/torso.png`. Keep the folder structure.
- Exact canvas sizes matter — the rig positions each part by its pivot, and the pivot is a fraction of the canvas.
- Magenta is keyed out on install. Do not deliver on white or transparent.
- Zip and send. `python3 tools/art-install.py <folder>` installs; the rig picks the parts up automatically and drops back to the whole-figure painting for any character whose set is incomplete.

**Acceptance test I run on receipt:** assemble the parts on the rig at rest and compare against the reference. If the figure matches, the pivots are right. If a limb floats away from its joint, that part's joint position was off and only that one part needs redoing.
