Survivors of the Worst Party

Keep them alive for ten minutes and the Demon King comes to you.

How to play

Move with WASD / arrows, or drag anywhere on the left of the screen.
Mend casts itself on the most wounded ally inside the small green circle, so walk to whoever is hurt. Your flask fills from the motes enemies drop; a full flask pays for a Revive or your ultimate.
Mend keeps them standing, but your prayers decide whether they win: Surge heals, Bless doubles the busiest ally’s damage, Haste speeds everyone up and keeps Brom awake, Fortify shields the party and makes Brom the only thing enemies can see, Rouse wakes, calms and shoves. R revives when someone falls and Q fires your ultimate, each for a full flask. P pauses. Keep two buffs rolling and the party is dangerous; let them lapse and it is not.
Three of twelve go with you, picked at camp. They stay inside your big circle, doing their own thing, so you can do yours; the small green circle is where your healing lands, so walk to the wounded (they come to you and shout when they are under half); more join as you meet the conditions on their cards. Each has a weapon, and a second one turns up as a level-up card once they have learned a little.
Every minute or so something happens: a shrine to hold, a bounty, a pilgrim to escort, a vigil over Brom, an ambush, a rain of motes, a champion to kill, a chest out in the dark, a stranger to heal, a blood moon, a spilled purse, or a member who refuses healing for thirty seconds (tap their card to hold off). See it through for a reward. The party levels up too, each in their own way, and picks their own skills: passives, and actives they cast on their own.
Wren’s prayers cast themselves: learn a spell on a level-up (Surge, Mending Prayer, Regrowth, Sanctuary, Fortify, Ward, Wall of Light, Bless, Haste, Rouse, Radiance) and it goes off on its own when the moment is right. The only button is Revive, which appears when the flask is full and costs all of it.
Your level-ups also turn up weapons — a bouncing ember, kitchen knives, a censer chain, orbiting hymnals, holy water, a fire ring, a poison trail, spinning blades, a mending circle, a static field, a gravity well, ball lightning, poison roots, a long slash, a whirlwind, a blizzard, a meteor shower, a boomerang axe, ghost hounds and more. Max one out and an Evolve card turns up — and you hand each one to a party member, who carries it from then on. Four at most, two per person, five ranks each. You still cannot attack.
Each member keeps a gratitude bar under their health: saving them when they are low fills it, leaving them hurt drains it. At 70 they give you something back (Pip stops hitting Brom, Lupa stops biting, Vex spares the party…) and at 100 they say thank you like they mean it.
Party members also pick quirks as they level: Colossus, Pyromaniac, Twin, Glass Cannon and more. Three per member per run, and no two runs come out the same.
Every card rolls a tier: Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary or Mythic, on level-ups and from chests. Higher tiers grant more ranks at once; a Mythic maxes the card and heals everyone.
Ghouls burrow and come up under someone. Wisps ignore the party and drain your flask on touch; keep away or let Pip shoot them.
Chests fall at 3:00 and 7:00, the Demon Knight drops one, and any kill might. Walk over a chest to pick one of three relics for the run. Hold both halves of a pair and the next chest fuses them into something better.
From two minutes in, elites arrive with an aura and one trick each: swift, armored, healer-hunting, bursting into imps, or singing Brom to sleep. Hexers lob bolts at you from a distance; keep moving.
Enemies that get close to a healer forget everything else. Brom snores. Pip panics. Vex casts through you. Nobody says thank you.
